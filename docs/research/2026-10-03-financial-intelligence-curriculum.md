FINDINGS
- OECD/INFE adults 2016: four content areas "A Money and transactions, B Planning and managing finances, C Risk and reward, D Financial landscape". A: money and currencies, income, payments/prices/purchases, financial records and contracts. B: budgeting, managing income and expenditure, saving, investing, longer-term planning and asset building, retirement, credit, debt management. C: identifying risk, financial safety nets and insurance, balancing risk and reward. D: regulation and consumer protection, rights and responsibilities, education/information/advice, products and services, scams and fraud, taxes and public spending, external influences. Three columns per topic: awareness/knowledge/understanding; skills/behaviour; confidence/motivation/attitudes. Order "does not indicate the relative difficulty".
- EU/OECD youth 2023: same four domains ("l'argent et les transactions, la planification et la gestion des finances, le risque et le rendement, le paysage financier"); age bands "6-10 ans, 11-15 ans et 16-18 ans", cumulative; cross-cutting: digital, sustainable finance, citizenship, entrepreneurship, entering adulthood; domain 3 adds the risk-return link and "la diversification" for older ages.
- PREF-UEMOA (BCEAO 2019 deck): no themes/modules listed; objective "connaissances requises en matière de finance de base"; targets: children 3-18, young workers 18-35, women, employees, rural people, elderly, SMEs; definition "informer, sensibiliser et former... sur les concepts financiers de base... à chaque étape de leur vie". Press 22 Jun 2026 quoting BCEAO annual report: "186 leçons et modules d'éducation financière ont été développés"; "25 langues ... le français, le portugais et vingt-trois langues locales"; "la création d'un site internet dédié" (no URL).
- Aflatoun: "Cinq Éléments de Base : 1. Exploration et Compréhension Personnelle 2. Droits et Responsabilités 3. Épargne et Dépense 4. Planification et Budgétisation 5. Entreprise Sociale et/ou Financière".
- FDIC Money Smart for Young Adults, 12 modules in order: Bank On It; Setting Goals and Making Financial Decisions; Making the Most of Your Income; Your Spending and Saving Plan; Saving for Your Goals and Your Future; Building Your Credit History; Borrowing Basics; Charge It Right; Protecting Your Money and Your Identity; Buying a Car; Paying for Education and Training; Living on Your Own.
- CFPB toolkit, 10 categories: Starting the Money Conversation; Setting Goals; Saving; Tracking Income and Benefits; Paying Bills; Getting through the Month; Dealing with Debt; Understanding Credit Reports and Scores; Choosing Financial Products and Services; Protecting your Money.
- NGPF, 11 units: Banking; Behavioral Economics; Budgeting; Career; Consumer Skills; Insurance; Investing; Paying for College; Taxes; Types of Credit; Managing Credit; mini-units incl. Cryptocurrency, Entrepreneurship, Gambling and Sports Betting.
